I implemented `parse_price` in `price.py` to follow its docstring, and the checks pass.

**How it works:**
1. It trims leading and trailing spaces and converts full-width digits to regular ones.
2. It removes a leading `-` (and remembers the price is negative), then a leading `¥`, then a trailing `円`.
3. What's left must be either plain digits, or 1–3 digits followed by groups of `,` plus exactly 3 digits. Anything else raises `ValueError`. That covers misplaced commas and strings with no digits at all.

**Checks:**
- The existing `test_price.py` prints `ok`.
- I also ran 9 examples from the docstring and similar ones (such as `" 12 "`, `"12,345,678"`, `"１２３"`, `"-¥5"` and `"¥1,234円"`). All 9 returned the expected number.
- I ran 15 inputs that should be rejected (such as `""`, `"¥"`, `"abc"`, `"1,23"`, `"1234,567"` and `"123,"`). All 15 raised `ValueError`.

**Choices where the docstring is silent:**
- `"¥1,234円"`, with both the yen sign and 円, is accepted.
- `"¥-5"` (minus after the yen sign), `"+5"` and digits from other scripts, such as Arabic `"١٢٣"`, are rejected.
- The full-width yen sign `￥` and full-width commas are not accepted.

I didn't add any test files; the extra checks were one-off commands.
